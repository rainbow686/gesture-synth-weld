🔍 What is a hand synthesizer?

A hand synthesizer is exactly what the name says: a synthesizer whose controller is your hand rather than a keybed. A sensor watches where your hands are and how many fingers are extended, and software turns those readings into musical decisions — which chord, which key, how loud, how bright.

The idea exists because a keyboard is a physical commitment: a fixed set of keys in a fixed order that only responds to being pressed. A hand is a continuous controller — it has position, height, rotation and five independently countable fingers — so a good mapping can pull harmony and expression out of two hands at once, with nothing to press.

What it is not: a replacement for a piano. Nothing beats a keybed for playing written music precisely. A hand synthesizer covers the other case — you want something musical right now, standing up, with the camera that is already in the room.

🎛 How two hands become a chord

The hard part of a hand synthesizer is not tracking — it's the mapping. Ten fingers give more gestural range than a musical parameter set needs, and a mapping that asks for too much at once is unplayable. The one on this page splits the job in two, and keeps each hand's job narrow enough to do without thinking.

HandGestureMusical result
Left
harmony		1 – 5 fingersScale degrees I – V in the chosen key
Index + pinkyDegree VI
Index + pinky + thumbDegree VII
Wrist tiltMajor / minor (Scale+Tilt mode)
Right
expression		Finger countChord thickness: 1 triad · 2 inversion · 3 seventh · 4 ninth
HeightVolume
TiltTone — the filter opening and closing
FistOctave shift; the left fist mutes

Two rules make it work. First, harmony is discrete and expression is continuous: finger counts snap to a scale degree, so you cannot play out of key, while height and tilt stay free so the sound never feels quantised. Second, both hands are required — which sounds restrictive but is exactly why it is playable, because it removes the "which hand does what" decision from every moment.

The move-by-move version, with the full gesture tables, is in the tutorial.

⚡ Thirty seconds to your first chord

1

Open the camera

Press play and allow camera access. The hand-tracking model downloads once (about 20 MB) and is cached after that; it runs entirely on your device.

2

Set the key

Pick a key from C to B — A is the default. Leave it alone while you learn: every finger count already resolves to a chord inside that key.

3

Left hand counts

One finger for I, two for ii, and so on. Change the count and the chord changes — that is the entire harmony control.

4

Right hand shapes

Raise it to get louder, tilt it to open the filter, add fingers to thicken the chord into a seventh or a ninth.

⚖ Hand synthesizer vs keyboard vs MIDI controller

All three make the same kind of sound. They differ in what the interface lets you express quickly.

If you already play keys, a hand synthesizer is not a substitute — it is a second instrument with a different trick: chords you can hear and change at arm's length, with both hands free. If you don't play keys, that is the point — there is no chord shape to memorise, only a finger count.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

How hands map ↑

What is a hand synthesizer?

A synthesizer you play with your hands instead of a keyboard. A sensor — usually a webcam, sometimes a glove or a ring — reads where your hands are and how many fingers are up, and software maps those positions onto musical parameters: pitch, volume, tone, chord type. Nothing is pressed; everything is moved.

Can you play a synthesizer without a keyboard?

Yes, three common ways: a theremin-style instrument where distance from a sensor sets pitch and volume; a MIDI controller with pads, breath or ribbons; or a hand-tracking synthesizer that reads gestures from a camera. The last one needs no hardware at all beyond the webcam already in your laptop or phone — that is the one on this page.

Do hand synthesizers need special hardware?

Not the camera-based kind. Glove and ring synthesizers cost $100–$1,000 and need drivers, calibration and charging. A webcam hand synthesizer uses the camera you already own, so the hardware cost is zero — the trade-off is that it needs reasonable light and your hands inside the frame.

How does a camera-based hand synthesizer work?

A hand-tracking model — this page uses MediaPipe — locates 21 landmarks per hand on every video frame. Those coordinates are smoothed, then mapped to music. Here the left hand chooses harmony (finger count selects a scale degree, wrist tilt picks major or minor) and the right hand shapes expression (height is volume, tilt is tone, finger count sets chord thickness). All of it runs on your device; the video never leaves the browser.

Is a hand synthesizer the same thing as a theremin?

No, though they share an idea. A theremin is a single continuous voice driven by two antennas — you can glide pitch, but you cannot easily play a chord. A hand synthesizer with finger tracking can select discrete harmony (a key and a scale degree) with one hand while the other shapes expression, which is what makes chords practical.

How long does it take to learn a hand synthesizer?

The first note takes about thirty seconds: allow the camera, raise both hands, and the instrument sounds. Chords are the second step — one to five fingers select scale degrees I to V, and two special gestures reach VI and VII. Most people are playing a recognisable progression within a few minutes.

Can I record what I play on a hand synthesizer?

Yes, up to 120 seconds per take. Three capture modes: audio only, full video with the camera feed and the hand skeleton drawn over it, or skeleton animation on a plain background if you would rather not show your room. Recording saves to your device — nothing is uploaded.

Is this hand synthesizer free?

Does a browser hand synthesizer work on a phone or tablet?

It does, but a laptop or desktop is the better experience: the camera sits at a comfortable distance, the screen is big enough to read the chord display, and the processor keeps up with tracking more reliably. On mobile the page works in portrait, and the browser's "Add to Home Screen" gives you a one-tap icon.

Can I build my own hand synthesizer?

Yes. This one is open source — MediaPipe hand tracking for input, Tone.js for the synth engine, React and TypeScript for the interface. Fork it on GitHub and swap the mapping if you want a different gesture vocabulary.
